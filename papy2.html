Software Engineering (26-27)

Course Description

Students in this honors course gain a deeper understanding of the end-to-end software engineering process, including development methodologies and project management, by designing and completing independent or collaborative projects of their choosing. Throughout their work, they explore the use of AI in the development process and examine the ethical implications of computing technologies. Students are also introduced to a range of fundamental computer science topics including data representation, computer architecture, networking, and operating systems. This college-level course prepares students for university study in computer science and engineering and provides hands-on experience with industry-standard tools.

This course addresses three tracks in parallel: Software Engineering Topics, Core Computer Science Topics, and Independent Projects.

Software Engineering Topics

These are topics in Software Engineering that provide students a deeper understanding of the end-to-end process of developing software. These topics will enhance students’ ability to work with stakeholders and manage and deliver larger projects.

Topics to include:

  • System Planning
  • System Design
  • Testing
  • Project Management
Core Computer Science Topics

These are topics in Computer Science that provide a solid foundation of knowledge for students. Covering this broad range of topics will prepare students for further studies in Computer Science.

Topics to include:

  • Binary Number Systems
  • Binary Representation
  • Computer Architecture
  • Operating Systems
  • Virtual Memory
  • Compilers
  • Databases
  • Data Science
  • Networking
  • Web Architecture
  • Control Systems
Independent Projects

Students will propose and execute independent or collaborative projects designed to advance their software engineering skills along with developing domain-specific knowledge.

Grading and Assessments

This course will use a range of assessments. These include written assessments, documentation, presentations, and demonstrations that all are designed to measure understanding and a student’s ability to apply their knowledge.

Learning Goals
  • Conceptual Understanding (30%)
    • Ability to demonstrate knowledge of course concepts
    • Ability to demonstrate knowledge of project domain

  • Technical Application (20%)
    • Ability to apply course concepts
    • Ability to apply knowledge of project domain

  • Design (20%)
    • Ability to apply the design process to project work

  • Communication (30%)
    • Ability to communicate technical concepts
    • Ability to communicate domain-specific concepts
